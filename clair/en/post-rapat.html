A complete guide to international funeral transport, death formalities and funeral pre-planning in Switzerland

Introduction

When a death occurs far from the country of origin, one of the first concerns of bereaved families is to organise the repatriation of the body of their loved one. In Switzerland, and particularly in Geneva where a particularly international population lives, this process is common. Funeral directors are then the essential contacts to coordinate all administrative formalities, preservation care, transport and burial in the country of origin.

This complete guide informs you about how much a body repatriation from Switzerland costs, the steps to follow, the documents required, and how funeral pre-planning can help you anticipate these expenses. All information on services and prices is based on the expertise of Murith funeral services, established in Geneva since 1888. Also discover the history of Maison Murith.

What is a body repatriation?

Body repatriation, also called international funeral transport or repatriation of mortal remains, refers to all the operations needed to carry the body of a deceased person from the place of death to the country or city where they will be buried or cremated. In Switzerland, this procedure is governed by strict regulations, involving funeral directors, cantonal authorities, consulates and transport companies.

The term "repatriation" is often used when the deceased is a foreign national and their family wishes to hold the funeral in their native country. It is also referred to as:

  • International funeral transport: a generic term for any movement of a body between two countries
  • Funeral convoy: transport of the coffin by land or air
  • Repatriation of remains: a common expression in consular procedures
  • Return of the deceased: wording used by some families and associations
  • Transport of a corpse or transfer of a body: official administrative terms used in legal documents

What factors influence the price of repatriation?

Several elements determine the final cost of international funeral transport from Switzerland. Understanding these factors will help you better anticipate expenses and make the decisions best suited to your situation.

1. The mode of transport

Air transport is the most common for long distances, but it imposes specific constraints, particularly regarding the coffin approved for air transport. Road transport, possible for nearby European destinations, can allow a family member to accompany the deceased in the funeral vehicle.

2. Body preservation care

During a repatriation, preservation care, also known as thanatopraxy or embalming, is often mandatory, particularly for air transport. This care consists of administering hygiene treatments to the body of the deceased in order to preserve it in the best possible conditions until arrival at the destination. Its cost ranges between CHF 800 and 2,500 depending on the services carried out.

3. The coffin and funeral equipment

The type of coffin used for an international repatriation meets precise standards. Airlines require approved coffins, often in welded zinc, surrounded by a second wooden packaging. Murith Care Everywhere funeral services offer a wide range of coffins suited to different regulatory and cultural requirements.

4. Administrative formalities

Repatriating a body from Switzerland involves many mandatory administrative procedures. Murith funeral services takes care of all of these steps in coordination with the competent authorities.

  1. Obtaining the death certificate from the Geneva civil registry department
  2. Contact with the consulate or embassy of the destination country
  3. Obtaining the mortuary pass (international transport permit)
  4. Coordination with the cantonal health authorities
  5. Organising reception on arrival by local funeral directors
  6. Booking the transport (flight or road convoy)

Essential documents for a repatriation

Assembling the administrative file is a crucial stage of repatriation. Any delay in providing documents can postpone the transport. Murith funeral services take charge of coordinating these steps by contacting the relevant authorities, consulate and embassy.

The documents generally required for a body repatriation from Switzerland are as follows:

  • The official death certificate issued by the Geneva or cantonal civil registry
  • The medical death certificate stating the cause of death
  • The international mortuary pass (or burial permit)
  • The deceased's passport or identity card
  • The burial authorisation issued by the authorities of the destination country
  • The certificate of hermetic sealing of the coffin signed by the doctor
  • The certificate of preservation care (thanatopraxy) if carried out
  • The transport documents (air waybill or road documents)

Funeral pre-planning: anticipating the cost of a repatriation

Faced with the high costs a body repatriation can represent, many people living in Switzerland, notably foreign nationals living in Geneva, choose to take out funeral pre-planning. This contract, concluded during one's lifetime, makes it possible to prepare one's funeral and ensure its financing in the event of death, including in the case of repatriation to the country of origin.

Frequently asked questions about body repatriation in Switzerland

Here are the questions most often asked of funeral directors when a death requires international funeral transport from Geneva or from other Swiss cantons.

Who can organise a body repatriation in Switzerland?

Only approved funeral directors can organise the international transport of a deceased person. Maison Murith, with more than 135 years of experience in Geneva, maintains privileged relationships with foreign diplomatic representations in Switzerland and with airlines, which allows fast and efficient handling.

How long does a body repatriation take?

The total duration of a repatriation depends on how long it takes to obtain the administrative documents and on transport availability. On average, allow between 5 and 15 days from death to burial in the country of origin.

Does health insurance or travel insurance cover repatriation?

Some travel insurance or supplementary health insurance policies may cover all or part of the repatriation costs. It is advisable to check the clauses of your insurance contract as soon as the death occurs. Murith funeral services can advise you on the steps to take with your insurer.

Can a ceremony be held in Geneva before the deceased leaves?

Yes, absolutely. Murith funeral services have a chapel and viewing rooms in the heart of Geneva, accessible 24/7, allowing a farewell ceremony to be held in quiet reflection before the deceased is repatriated to their country of origin. This ceremony can be secular or religious, according to the family's wishes.

Burial and funeral: the possible choices

When a deceased person is repatriated to their native country, the funeral and burial are organised on site by local funeral directors, in coordination with the Geneva funeral directors who handled the transport. Two main options are available to the family in terms of method of burial:

  • Burial (interment): the coffin containing the body of the deceased is placed in a grave in the cemetery. It is the most traditional form of burial, practised in many cultures and religions.
  • Cremation (incineration): the body is cremated and the ashes are given to the family in a funeral urn. This option can be considered before or after the repatriation, depending on the family's preferences and local regulations.

In some cases, families also choose to have the ashes repatriated rather than the body, which considerably simplifies the administrative formalities and reduces transport costs.

How to reduce the cost of a body repatriation?

While the cost of a repatriation may seem high, several solutions make it possible to anticipate or reduce it. Here are the main avenues to explore:

  1. Take out funeral pre-planning during your lifetime: it is the most effective solution. It makes it possible to set the arrangements and financing of the funeral before death.
  2. Check your travel or life insurance cover: some contracts include body repatriation. Read your insurance policies carefully.
  3. Contact community associations: some expatriate or cultural associations offer assistance or mutual funds covering repatriation costs.
  4. Opt for cremation before repatriation: if the family and religion allow it, repatriating ashes is significantly less costly than transporting a body.
  5. Compare quotes: do not hesitate to ask funeral directors for a detailed quote. Maison Murith makes this easy on its website.
