Introduction:

Losing a loved one is one of the most painful experiences of life. When grieving, the relatives of the deceased often have to organise a dignified and respectful funeral within a very short time. In Switzerland, funeral directors play a central role in supporting bereaved families, guiding them step by step through every procedure, from taking charge of the deceased to burial or cremation, including organising the funeral ceremony.

Since April 1888, Murith funeral services, based at Boulevard de la Cluse 89 in Geneva, opposite the Cantonal Hospital (HUG), have offered complete and fully personalised funeral services in Geneva. Adapted to religious convictions, the wishes of the deceased and the needs of each family, the services of Maison Murith cover the organisation of funerals, body preservation care (thanatopraxy), international transport and repatriations, funeral pre-planning, as well as the provision of viewing rooms accessible 24/7. This complete guide aims to support you through this difficult time, giving you all the essential information about funerals in Switzerland: administrative steps, types of ceremonies, choice of burial, funeral costs and pre-planning options.

In Switzerland, funeral regulations vary from canton to canton. In some cantons, private Swiss funeral companies handle all organisational and administrative tasks. In others, the municipality plays a predominant role. In several regions, responsibilities are shared between the public authority and private providers. Whichever canton you live in, the history of Murith funeral services bears witness to more than 135 years of experience serving families, with a team of skilled and deeply human professionals.

  1. Post-Funeral Steps: Funeral Directors Switzerland

Once the funeral is over, the heirs must complete a number of administrative formalities. Murith funeral services in Geneva can direct you to the right contacts, or even support you through these steps:

  • Closing bank accounts and notifying financial institutions
  • Cancelling contracts (phone subscription, insurance, rental lease, various subscriptions)
  • Contacting a notary to manage the estate and the division of assets
  • Cancelling the deceased's AVS/AHV card and identity documents
  • Applying for a widow's or widower's pension from the social insurance schemes
  • Dealing with the employer of the deceased if they were still working
  • Registering the heirs in the land registry on presentation of a certificate of inheritance
  • Filing a tax return effective from the date of death

  1. Types of Funerals and Ceremonies in Switzerland

The organisation of funerals in Switzerland offers great flexibility to honour the memory of the deceased in a personalised way. Maison Murith supports you in choosing between two main burial options: burial (interment in a cemetery) and cremation. In addition to these methods, there are various types of Swiss funeral ceremonies, adapted to everyone's convictions and wishes.

Burial in a Cemetery

Burial, or interment, is an ancient funeral rite that consists of placing the coffin containing the remains of the deceased in a grave dug into the ground of a cemetery. In Switzerland, this practice remains a respected option deeply rooted in religious and cultural traditions, notably in Catholicism and in certain Muslim, Jewish and Orthodox communities.

The grave may be marked by a headstone, a cross, a plaque or another funeral monument chosen by the family. The funeral concession is granted by the municipality for a fixed period, generally renewable. Costs vary by city: in Geneva, a 20-year concession costs around CHF 2,700; in Lausanne, a 30-year concession costs CHF 1,800; in Fribourg, it is CHF 1,950 for 30 years. Maison Murith coordinates with the municipal authorities and handles all the logistical aspects of the burial.

In the case of burial, the main costs include the coffin as well as the acquisition of a funeral concession in Geneva, generally valid for 20 years and renewable.

Cremation

Cremation is today the most common method of burial in Switzerland. According to estimates from the Swiss funeral sector, nearly 90% of deceased people opt for cremation. After cremation, the ashes are placed in an urn that the family can place in a columbarium, bury in a cemetery or, under certain legal conditions, scatter in nature. Murith funeral services offer a choice of urns suited to every wish and budget.

Even in the case of cremation, a coffin is mandatory in Switzerland for transferring the deceased to the crematorium. Murith funeral services Geneva offer many woods and finishes of coffins, all European-made and of high quality: fir, poplar, oak, mahogany, carved walnut, ash, frake and many others. Our funeral advisors are at your disposal to help you make the best choice.

  1. Funeral Ceremonies: Religious or Secular?

Funeral organisation in Switzerland offers two main options for the ceremony: the religious ceremony and the secular ceremony (civil or humanist). Murith funeral services specialise in personalising ceremonies of all faiths: Catholic, Protestant, Muslim, Jewish, Orthodox, or entirely secular. Our experienced masters of ceremonies harmonise the family's choices with their traditions, for a unique and dignified tribute.

The Religious Ceremony

Deeply rooted in Swiss cultural tradition, the religious ceremony provides a spiritual setting to say goodbye to the deceased. It comforts relatives through the dimension of faith and established rites. Maison Murith works with all religious and civil structures, in complete discretion:

  • Catholicism: funeral mass, prayers, liturgical chants, intervention of a priest or deacon
  • Protestantism: funeral service at the church, sermon, hymns and prayers
  • Islam: prompt funeral, coffin oriented towards Mecca, ritual ablutions
  • Judaism: funeral without flowers, specific prayers, burial within 24 hours if possible
  • Orthodoxy: liturgical ceremony, coffin oriented towards the east, specific rites according to the Church

The Secular Ceremony

The secular ceremony, also called a civil ceremony or farewell ceremony, makes no religious reference. It is centred on the life and personality of the deceased. Maison Murith, particularly attached to the human dimension, specialises in organising these personalised ceremonies, which are growing in popularity in French-speaking Switzerland. Because every tribute is unique, our teams support you attentively to personalise every detail: from flowers to your favourite music, from testimonials to video projections in our chapel.

The typical course of a secular ceremony includes testimonials from relatives, contributions from the celebrant, readings (poems, personal texts, literary extracts), music chosen according to the deceased's tastes, and a moment of reflection or silence. Our La Cluse chapel in Geneva can welcome around sixty people with the possibility of video projection.

In French-speaking Switzerland, secular celebrants and officiants are grouped within the Association des célébrant.e.s et officiant.e.s romand.e.s (ACOR). Their fees are generally between CHF 900 and CHF 1,200 for a complete funeral ceremony.

Assistance and Free Services

In Switzerland, some forms of financial assistance exist for families who cannot bear the cost of a funeral alone. The City of Geneva, for example, provides free basic funeral services for residents domiciled within its territory at the time of their death, under certain conditions. This free provision notably includes transport, the funeral procession, the use of a municipal funeral chapel, cremation and the supply of a standard urn.

Other municipalities and cantons also offer similar assistance for people in need. Murith funeral services Geneva direct you to the right contacts and inform you of the assistance available in your municipality or canton.

What is Funeral Pre-Planning?

Funeral pre-planning, also called a funeral pre-arrangement contract or funeral agreement, is a contract signed between an adult and Murith funeral services. This document records all of the person's wishes for their funeral: type of ceremony, celebrant (religious or secular), choice of music, coffin, place of burial, place for scattering the ashes, and so on. Thanks to a personal online space, the family benefits from complete transparency on payment information and contractual data.

Funeral pre-planning at Murith funeral services is fundamentally different from funeral insurance. It is a savings contract, open to any adult resident in Switzerland, with no health questionnaire and no maximum age limit. Financing can be made in one go or by instalments.

Maison Murith guarantees strict respect for the wishes of the deceased, in accordance with the quote drawn up, while offering an accessible and economical alternative.

  12. Tributes and Condolences: How to Support Grieving Families

When a loved one dies, the people who were dear to them look for ways to express their support and condolences to the bereaved family. In Switzerland, several traditions and practices help to support relatives in their grief and pay tribute to the person who has passed away.

Condolences

Expressing condolences is an essential gesture of solidarity and humanity. In Switzerland, condolences can be expressed in several ways:

  • In person, at the funeral ceremony or the wake
  • By a written message (letter of condolence, card, email)
  • By sending flowers or a floral arrangement to the family or the cemetery
  • By a donation to a charity chosen by the family, in memory of the deceased
  • By attending the funeral meal (refreshments) held after the funeral

  1. Cantonal Specificities: Focus on Geneva and French-speaking Switzerland

Switzerland is a federal country whose cantons have great autonomy in funeral regulation. This diversity results in notable differences in the organisation and financing of funerals from one canton to another. Here is an overview of the specificities of a few French-speaking cantons.

Geneva: A Historic Municipal Service and Maison Murith

The City of Geneva has had a municipal funeral service since 1866. This service handles funerals, burials and cremations of people who die in the Geneva area. Free basic funeral services are granted to residents under certain conditions. Murith funeral services, founded in Geneva in 1888 and located at Boulevard de la Cluse 89, is a leading private alternative for families wanting more personalised funerals, thanatopraxy care, tailor-made ceremonies, international transport, or complete care including administrative formalities and funeral pre-planning.

The Canton of Vaud

In the canton of Vaud, cremation is the norm, often carried out at the Montoie Crematorium in Lausanne. Funeral pre-planning is seen there as a pragmatic act of planning aimed at relieving one's loved ones. The network of Vaud funeral directors is dense, with family businesses holding the federal diploma present in all major localities.

Valais, Fribourg and Neuchâtel

Valais presents a fascinating contrast between its urban areas, where cremation is the majority choice (as in Sion), and certain mountain valleys where burial remains a strong tradition. Fribourg, a bilingual canton, offers funeral services adapted to both language communities. Neuchâtel, at the forefront of modernity, is seeing digital and flexible funeral pre-planning solutions emerge, in line with the expectations of a young urban population.
