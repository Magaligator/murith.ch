The milestones of our history

1888

It all began in April 1888

At the insistent request of the Geneva clergy, an agreement creating the company Pompes Funèbres Murith SA was signed.

With the help of Messrs Michel Fert and François Duparc, both coachmen, Anselme Murith, then sacristan of the church of Saint-Joseph, ran the business, which was incorporated on 28 April 1888. In 1913 he was assisted by his sons Paul and Auguste. On 14 September 1898, it fell to Anselme to organise the funeral procession of Her Majesty Empress Sissi of Austria.

1919

In 1919

On the initiative of Paul Murith, the company A. MURITH SA, a specialist funeral company, was created. Under his leadership, the company expanded into the canton of Fribourg with his brother Auguste Murith, and into Valais.

1960

From 1960 to 1981

Mr Henri Murith, son of Auguste, took over the management. With his training as a cabinetmaker, a workshop for making coffins came into being. Other funeral products better suited to demand were also made during this period.

1981

From 1981 to 2020

Mr Jean Murith, son of Henri, ran the company. He was the driving force behind the construction of the building that houses the current premises. Funeral chapels could thus be created, enhancing the spaces of reflection available to families.

This was also the beginning of preservation care (thanatopraxy) in the company, thanks to Jean. As the coffin workshop was no longer profitable given its costs, the decision was taken more than 25 years ago to import quality coffins, in line with the expectations of the families who call on our services.

Today

Today

Jacques, Philippe and Jean-Marc now run the company. Three brothers with the mission of preserving the identity of the business built over all these generations, while bringing modernity to ceremonies and the technologies of our time. This is how Maison Murith specialises in personalising civil ceremonies and/or ceremonies of all faiths.

Particularly attached to the human dimension, we hope to contribute a stone to the chain of mutual support that, from healthcare staff to administrations, assists bereaved families.
