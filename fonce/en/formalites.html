Declaring the death

The death must be declared within two days to the civil registrar of the district where it occurred. If the death took place in a hospital, clinic, detention facility or any similar organisation, the declaration is the responsibility of its director.

Death certificate

This is an extract from the death register (not to be confused with the medical death certificate issued by a doctor), which can be obtained from the Civil Registry Office of the place of death. In addition to details about the deceased, it states where and when the death occurred, but not the causes. It may be required, for example, by an insurer or a bank to prove the death. It is advisable to obtain several copies.

Preparing the funeral

Murith funeral services will take care of all the formalities linked to the death and to organising the funeral, such as transporting the body, writing the death notice for the press, providing a place for quiet reflection and coordinating all parties involved.

Will

Any will found at the time of death must be handed without delay to the competent authority; this also applies to wills whose validity is doubtful or that appear to have been revoked. An heir who breaches this obligation risks being excluded from the estate.

What are the administrative steps following a death?

Administrative formalities in Geneva require the declaration of the death, obtaining a death certificate and contacting financial institutions to block and unblock the deceased's accounts. Our funeral service takes care of everything related to the deceased and the administrative steps.

What are the steps in preparing the body?

Preparing the body is a crucial stage in organising a funeral. Murith funeral services, based in Geneva, offer specialised services for preparing the deceased, including mortuary care, dressing and placing in the coffin.
